Quick Heating and Cooling Help in Memphis

When your system quits, how quickly help arrives matters more than anything else. For that reason, we keep scheduling flexible across the area and put a real person on the phone. Emergency calls usually get a technician within a day, and we treat a failed furnace in a cold snap or a dead AC in the heat as top priority.

The emergency calls we run tend to follow a pattern. On the cooling side, it's an AC blowing warm air, an outdoor unit that hums without starting, or an evaporator coil iced over. On the heating side, it's a furnace that ignites and drops out. And any time of year, it's breakers that trip whenever the system starts, plugged condensate drains backing water into the furnace cabinet, and thermostats that stop responding. Because our trucks are stocked with capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and fittings, the majority of these calls are wrapped up in one visit, with the price agreed before any work begins.

Two common situations call for a quick step from you before a technician arrives. The first is an iced-over air conditioner: turn the system off at the thermostat and set the fan to "on" so the moving air thaws the coil, because running it frozen can flood the drain pan and damage the compressor. The second is a furnace that keeps cycling on and off or produces a burning smell. Shut it down at the switch and give us a call. Those few minutes can prevent a much larger repair.

Getting More Years From Your HVAC System in Memphis

Many mid-season breakdowns start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Change your filter every one to three months, clear vegetation at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and hose off the condenser coil once a year in spring. Then book a professional tune-up twice a year, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular service, a central air conditioner usually runs 12 to 15 years, a furnace 15 to 20, and a heat pump 10 to 15. Once a system passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like rising energy bills, rooms that never reach the setpoint, or repeat service calls, it's time to plan a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap during the worst heat wave of the year.

Minor issues rarely stay small when they're ignored. Take a weak capacitor: swapping it out is a quick, inexpensive repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and suddenly a minor fix becomes a full system replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to save our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a few miserable nights.
